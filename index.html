Learn Value Investing with Phoebe

Value investing, in the Graham and Buffett sense, is a way of reasoning before it is a way of buying: keep the price of a share and the value of a business in two different columns, estimate the value as a range, and leave a gap. These sixteen sessions teach it twice. The Owner track does it without a spreadsheet, on a constructed family business. The Analyst track does it on four large US companies' real 10-K filings, from owner earnings to a discounted cash flow to the growth a price implies. The bench in the last session checks five forecasting rules against what the filings later showed: holding out the last five years, the five-year average of owner earnings missed by 47.4 percent and last year's earnings by 70.4, and the rule a hopeful analyst reaches for, compounding the best year's growth, missed by 19,614.8 percent. No market prices, no tickers, no tips. Educational, not investment advice.

Owner track 🧭 · six sessions, no code

For anyone who wants to think about a business the way an owner does. What a share is, why value is a range, the margin of safety, the edge of what you know, Mr Market, and the mistakes made in your own head. The running case is Halden Tiles, a constructed family business; every Halden figure is invented and labelled so.

Analyst track 🔬 · ten sessions, browser and Python

For the people who build the number and have to defend it. Owner earnings from tagged 10-K facts, normalising through a cycle, return on capital, a discounted cash flow by hand and in reverse, sensitivity, base rates of growth, a moat as a valuation input, and the memo that says what would prove it wrong. Four filers throughout: Caterpillar, Exxon Mobil, Johnson & Johnson and Procter & Gamble.
